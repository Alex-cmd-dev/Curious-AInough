# Activity: Verify a Fantasy Football Claim


## The scenario

You are deciding whether to start a player in a fantasy football lineup. Someone says, “Start [player] this week because [reason].” Practice checking whether the reason is supported by current, relevant evidence instead of taking a confident take or projection at face value.

## Before you ask

Write down the player, the claim you heard, and what you currently think. Avoid sharing private league details or account information. Fantasy projections are uncertain; this activity is about evaluating evidence, not guaranteeing an outcome.

## Copy this into your AI assistant

Copy the whole activity prompt below and paste it into the AI provider you already use. Stay in that chat to complete the activity and reflection.

```text
I want to practice verifying a fantasy football claim before making my own lineup decision. The claim is: “[player] should be started this week because [reason].” The relevant NFL week and season are: [week and season]. My format, if relevant, is: [for example, PPR or standard]. Do not make the lineup decision for me.

First ask what I currently think and what evidence might change my mind. Then help me investigate the claim without giving a verdict upfront. Suggest search terms and the kinds of sources that could answer it, such as official team injury reports, the NFL or team gamebook, and reputable statistical sources. Clearly distinguish reported facts, projections, and your own inference. Do not invent current news, statistics, citations, or quotes. If you cannot access current information, say so and help me identify what to look up myself.

Wait while I open at least two sources directly. Ask me to report each source's publisher, date, relevant evidence, and what specific part of the claim it supports. Help me compare the evidence and note limits: a projection is not a fact, a small sample can mislead, and a player's role, opponent, injury status, and scoring format may matter. Ask me to write a short conclusion that separates evidence from uncertainty and includes the sources I checked. Challenge my reasoning without deciding who I should start.

After the reflection, ask whether I would like you to create a similar exercise. If I say yes, use a different fantasy football claim, keep current facts and projections clearly separated, do not invent evidence, and have me inspect sources directly before drawing a conclusion.
```

## Do the activity

1. Replace the bracketed details with a claim you actually want to check. Do not provide account credentials or private league information.
2. Search for current information and open at least two sources yourself; don't rely on an AI summary or search snippet.
3. Record the source, publication or update date, what it says, and whether it supports the claim.
4. Separate confirmed information from projections and your own interpretation. Note anything still uncertain.
5. Decide for yourself whether the evidence changes your lineup thinking. No source or projection can guarantee a player's result.

## Check your understanding

Can you point to the evidence supporting the claim? Did you distinguish a current report from a projection? What uncertainty remains, and what would you want to verify closer to kickoff?
