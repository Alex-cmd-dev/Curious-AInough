## Example 1: Academic integrity

*Applies to all fields.*

**Scenario:** You have a difficult homework assignment and decide to use AI.

### What ethical use of AI could look like

- "I don't understand how to solve this type of problem. Explain the concept to me and walk me through a similar example."
- "Here's the paragraph I wrote. Can you point out grammar mistakes and explain how I could make it clearer?"

### What problematic use of AI could look like

- "Here is my assignment. Complete the entire thing for me so I can submit it."
- Using AI to answer questions during a quiz or exam when AI isn't permitted.

**Key idea:** Use AI to support your thinking, not replace your responsibility.

**A good rule:** check the rules for the class or assignment first. If you're unsure, ask the professor.

## Example 2: Company privacy and security

*Applies to engineering.*

**Scenario:** You're an engineering intern and you're having trouble analyzing some data from your company.

**Your initial solution:** Upload the Excel file to ChatGPT and ask it to analyze it.

### The problem

The spreadsheet may contain things like:

- Employee names
- Production numbers
- Internal processes
- Part numbers
- Customer information
- Unreleased designs
- Other company data

Even if your intention is harmless, uploading that information to an AI service your company hasn't approved could violate company privacy and security rules.

### A better approach

Before uploading anything, ask:

- Is this information mine to share?
- Is it confidential?
- Does my company allow this AI tool?
- Can I remove identifying or proprietary information?

Instead of uploading the company's actual production spreadsheet, you could remove confidential information or create fake or sample data, then ask ChatGPT:

> I have a dataset with columns for cycle time, station number, and production quantity. How could I analyze bottlenecks?

Now you're learning how to solve the problem without unnecessarily exposing company information.

**Key idea:** Don't give AI information simply because it would make your task easier. Make sure you actually have permission to share it.

## Tool to take with you: the 4-Question AI Check

Before using AI, ask:

1. **Can I share it?**
   Is it private, confidential, personal, copyrighted, or someone else's information?
2. **Am I allowed to use AI for this?**
   Check your professor's, employer's, or organization's rules.
3. **Can I verify it?**
   AI can make mistakes. Check important information before relying on it.
4. **Would I be comfortable explaining how I used AI?**
   If you'd be uncomfortable telling your professor, employer, or teammate what you did, reconsider how you're using it.
