# Activity: Learn Core Cooking Concepts With AI

[⬅ Back to Activities](README.md)

## The scenario

You want to understand what is happening when food cooks, so you can make better choices instead of just following steps. Explore how heat, time, moisture, and browning affect a simple meal. AI acts as a coach: you make predictions and explain your thinking before it offers feedback.

## Before you ask

Choose any meal you would like to understand better. It can be as simple as toast or as involved as a stir-fry. Write what you think would happen if one part of the meal were cooked briefly over high heat, then longer over lower heat. You do not need cooking experience to try.

## Copy this into your AI assistant

```text
Teach me core cooking concepts through this meal: [write any meal you choose]. I want to understand how heat, time, moisture, and browning affect the food, not just get a recipe.

Act as a patient coach. Ask me one question at a time. First ask me to predict what would happen if one part of this meal were cooked briefly over high heat versus longer over lower heat. Do not give me the explanation until I try.

After my attempt, give one hint at a time and explain the role of heat, time, and moisture in plain language. Explain that surface browning depends on the food and method; wet surfaces tend to steam until enough moisture evaporates, and food in water cannot get hotter than the boiling point of that liquid at normal conditions. Ask me to explain the idea back in my own words. Then give me a new situation—for example, a crowded pan, a wet ingredient, or a different cooking method—and ask me to predict what might change before you explain. Correct misunderstandings kindly. Distinguish general principles from details that depend on the food, pan, stove, or recipe. If my meal includes meat, poultry, seafood, or eggs, do not use color or timing alone to judge safety; direct me to current official food-safety guidance and a food thermometer where appropriate. Do not tell me to taste food that may be undercooked or unsafe.
```

## Do the activity

1. Make your first prediction before asking for an explanation.
2. Work through the hints and explain what you think heat, time, and moisture do.
3. Try the new situation without asking for the answer first. If you have a safe opportunity, observe someone preparing your chosen meal; this activity does not require you to use a stove.
4. Close the chat and write one principle you think you understand, one example that supports it, and one question you still have.

## Check your understanding

Can you explain why changing heat, time, moisture, or cooking method can change your chosen meal's texture or browning? Can you use the idea to make a prediction about another meal? If not, ask for another hint or example rather than memorizing a rule.

## Further reading

- [Exploratorium: Science of Cooking](https://annex.exploratorium.edu/cooking/): approachable explanations of how heat and water affect food.
- [FoodSafety.gov: Safe Minimum Internal Temperatures](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures): official guidance for cooking foods that need a safe internal temperature.
