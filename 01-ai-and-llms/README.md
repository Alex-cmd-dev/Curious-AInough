## The kind of AI people usually mean

Artificial intelligence (AI) is a broad term for computer systems that do tasks associated with human abilities, such as recognizing images, understanding speech, or generating language. Today, when people casually say “AI,” they often mean a chat assistant powered by a large language model (LLM), such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or Grok.

An LLM learns patterns in language and generates responses from the text and other context it receives. The assistant is the product you interact with; the LLM is one kind of model that can power it. These terms are often used interchangeably in everyday conversation, though they are not exactly the same. AI also includes many systems that are not LLMs, and chat assistants can combine an LLM with tools such as web search, file analysis, or image generation.

LLMs can be useful for explaining, drafting, learning, and exploring ideas. They can also produce confident mistakes, so check important claims and make your own decisions. This guide focuses mostly on using LLM-powered assistants well.

For a deeper explanation of how LLMs, tools, MCP, and agents work, see [Large Language Models](../10-large-language-models/README.md). To compare tools by task, see the [Tool Directory](../11-tool-directory/README.md).
